## New post

**Dispatch Nº 118 - "The shipping rate that ignores your box"**
`how-to-fix-shopify-shipping-rates-that-ignore-product-dimensions.html` · by Priya Singh · 2026-09-18 · 2,443 words (stated 2,300) · 10 min

Topic: Shopify has no product dimensions field, and quotes every carrier-calculated rate against a
single default package. A variant carries a weight and nothing else, so long, light and bulky
orders are priced on a number the carrier stopped billing on years ago. The post covers the data
model gap, the community record, the dimensional-weight arithmetic, the two-part bill, the API
walls, and the playbook.

Facts carried, all read live this run:

- Shopify moderator, February 5, 2021, on the first product-dimensions request: "Shopify doesn't
  have support for that. We use an app called boxify." Same app named again in a staff reply on
  March 9, 2024.
- That 2021 thread ran to 2023 and 20-plus replies: "How is that even possible!?!?! Every vendor
  has multiple products and multiple box sizes"; "If I list on etsy, eBay, Bonanza or anywhere
  else, I don't have to worry about this issue"; "WHY am I entering the dimensions to ship my
  items after the customer has already paid?"
- November 11, 2021 API-forum question on passing product length/height/width into a
  CarrierService rate request: never answered; re-asked April 2024, never answered.
- March 22, 2023: "$9.69 when the shipping was nearly $13." Staff: "the rates are provided via the
  carrier, not Shopify", discrepancies occur when "order dimensions are slightly larger than listed".
- September 15, 2023: "you can only designate a single default shipping box."
- January 21, 2024, the profane thread (14 replies): "they charge based on SIZE as well??"; "I am
  just in awe at the stupidity". Staff pointed at the default package setting, then at Boxify.
- February 22, 2024: "the calculation is completely wrong and under charging my customers due to
  lack of product dimensions"; "this should be a problem addressed by SHOPIFY not 3rd party apps".
- March 12, 2024, thread past 100 replies: "It is ridiculous that you need an app to do this", the
  "$20 dollar discrepancy on shipping for large and bulky items which was calculated with the
  default small box at checkout".
- January 31, 2025: "How on earth does it make sense for Shopify to only calculate shipping based
  on 1 default package... I can't charge accurate shipping prices." Replies through May 2025 are
  app names and monthly prices, one rebranded mid-thread.
- Carrier arithmetic: UPS and FedEx bill the greater of actual and dimensional weight at
  L x W x H / 139. Effective August 18, 2025 both round every fractional inch up before dividing
  (9.1 to 10, 5.01 to 6; an 11.1 x 8.5 x 6.2 box moved from 5 to 6 billable pounds). January 12,
  2026 (FedEx) and January 26, 2026 (UPS): additional handling over 10,368 cubic inches, oversize
  or large package over 17,280 cubic inches or 110 lb.
- Shopify's own label-billing documentation on the second charge: "A shipping label price
  adjustment occurs when you purchase a shipping label and the shipping carrier determines that
  you've provided inaccurate information"; one listed reason is that "the package dimensions
  specified on the label don't match the actual dimensions of shipment"; "your carrier credits or
  debits you the difference", shown on the next bill.
- Why the automation doors are shut: Flow reacts to events that already happened and there is no
  rate-request trigger; Delivery Customization Functions hide, rename and reorder only, they
  "cannot create an option" and "cannot change a price"; the Carrier Service API is the only route
  that computes, and its payload carries weights, not per-product dimensions.

Honest limits stated in the post and the FAQ: the tool cannot invent a measurement for a product
nobody has ever measured, so the first pass on a messy catalog needs a tape and a person, and a
rate returned through the Carrier Service API has to answer inside the checkout timeout.
