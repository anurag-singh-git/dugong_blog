# QA report - 2026-10-12

Scheduled autonomous run. Full-tree QA of blog.dugong.live.

**The tree moved under this run.** A separate scheduled session (an SEO/GEO pass) was writing to the
same folder while this QA was in progress: between the opening snapshot at 18:15 UTC and 18:29 UTC it
added eight topic-guide hub pages, a new dispatch, a new stub, expanded the BlogPosting JSON-LD and
breadcrumbs on ~85 pages, and rewired sitemap.xml, feed.xml and llms.txt. Nothing was clobbered in
either direction (every edit here was a read-modify-write against the live file, not a cached copy),
but the audit was run twice: once against the 158-file tree as found, and again against the settled
173-file tree after the other run went quiet. All figures below are the second pass. Seven defects
found and fixed. No facts, FAQ answers or headlines were changed.

## Method

- Structural audit of all 168 HTML files: tag and CSS brace balance, one h1 per rendered page,
  JSON-LD parses and field completeness, internal link targets, in-page anchors, asset references,
  canonical vs og:url, og:title vs h1, html lang, viewport, charset, meta description length and
  uniqueness, title length and uniqueness, duplicate ids, replacement characters and mojibake,
  unterminated entities, doubled words inside single text nodes, heading-level skips, img alt,
  stub noindex and redirect resolution, visible date vs JSON-LD datePublished, dispatch-number
  collisions, visible em and en dashes, UK spellings.
- Headless Chromium render of all 88 rendered pages at 320, 360, 375, 414, 768, 1024, 1280 and
  1920 px (704 renders per pass, three passes): horizontal page overflow, elements escaping the
  viewport, computed font size of every text-bearing element, broken images, tap-target height at
  phone widths, console and page errors.
- WCAG AA contrast on every text node of all 88 pages at all eight widths, each measured against its
  resolved composited background with alpha blending through ancestors.
- Dictionary spell-check of every article body (240 out-of-dictionary tokens reviewed by hand;
  all but three were domain jargon, deliberate typo examples such as `gmial.com`, or verbatim
  merchant quotes, which are left as written).
- Coverage cross-check: sitemap.xml, feed.xml, llms.txt and llms-full.txt against the file tree;
  sitemap lastmod and feed pubDate sanity, feed ordering, guid uniqueness.
- Link graph: inbound and outbound internal links per page, stub redirect resolution.

## Defects found and fixed

1. **The byline and hero separator dots were effectively invisible: 1.48:1 contrast.** `.byline-divider`
   and `.hero-divider` took their color from `--border-strong`, which is `rgba(15,15,12,.18)` - a
   border alpha being used as a text color. On the homepage hero this made the meta line read
   "ISSUE Nº 124 OCT 11 · 2026" instead of "ISSUE Nº 124 · OCT 11 · 2026", and the same dot was lost
   in every article byline and article-meta row. All 209 separator glyphs across 88 files now use
   `rgba(15,15,12,.45)` (3.03:1, visible but still subordinate to the text it separates) and carry
   `aria-hidden="true"`, which is the correct treatment for a decorative glyph and takes them out of
   the screen-reader stream and out of automated contrast audits. Nothing else used that token as a
   text color; its border uses are untouched.

2. **404.html had sub-12px text at every width.** The `.kicker` line ("ERROR 404 · DUGONG FIELD
   NOTES") was `.72rem` = 11.52 px, under the 12 px floor the rest of the tree has enforced on phones
   since the 10-11 run. 404.html carries its own stylesheet and was missed by that pass. Now `.75rem`.

3. **Eight new topic-guide hub pages shipped with eight dead links each.** The hub pages were
   generated from the homepage template, so they inherited its in-page navigation: `href="#"` on the
   brand mark and `href="#latest"`, `#tags`, `#archive` in the nav and footer. The homepage resolves
   those against its own section ids; a hub page has no such ids and no JavaScript, so all 64 links
   went nowhere. They now follow the article template exactly: `index.html` on the brand, nav links to
   `index.html#latest` / `#tags` / `#archive`, and footer links to `index.html`. The inert
   `data-nav` and `data-filter-link` attributes were dropped with them.

4. **Heading-level skip on all eight hub pages.** Each went h1 straight to the h3 card titles with no
   h2 between. The two section eyebrows on each page ("◆ 16 DISPATCHES IN THIS GUIDE", "◆ OTHER TOPIC
   GUIDES") are the section headings in fact, so they are now `h2.section-eyebrow`, with `margin: 0`
   added to that rule in the same files so the browser's default h2 margin cannot shift the flex row.
   Rendered and re-measured: identical layout, correct outline. Articles and the homepage keep the
   span, because there the eyebrows sit under an h1 that already has h2s beneath it.

5. **A visible en dash on all eight hub pages.** The article-meta date range read
   "JUN 05 &ndash; OCT 12 2026". The tree has carried zero visible em or en dashes for months; this is
   now an arrow, "JUN 05 → OCT 12 2026", which the design already uses elsewhere ("read the dispatch →").
   Tree-wide count of visible em and en dashes is back to zero.

6. **`.hub-more` was a 23 px tap target on phones.** The "more on <topic> →" link the SEO run added to
   78 article pages is a standalone inline-block link, so the WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 line-height exemption
   does not cover it. 5 px of vertical padding at ≤560 px brings it to 28 px. Desktop is untouched.

7. **UK spellings in a US-English tree.** 24 occurrences: `greyed` (13, across the pickup-orders post,
   the homepage, feed.xml, llms.txt and llms-full.txt, including the FAQ question and its JSON-LD
   twin, which were changed together so the two still match), `fulfilment` (11, all in the new
   committed-inventory dispatch and its llms-full.txt copy, against 753 existing uses of
   "fulfillment"), `labelled` (4), `unlabelled` (6) and `maths` (2, against 100+ uses of "math").
   `analyses` was left alone: it is correct US English. The merchant quote that reads "I dont
   understand why they would even allow duplicate a product if you cant..." is reproduced verbatim
   and was left as written.

## Standing items (not changed)

1. index.html and 404.html keep an og:title and og:description that differ from their h1 and meta
   description. Both are hand-written site-level blurbs well inside the length caps, and were left as
   written rather than flattened to match the per-article convention.
2. **Nothing is committed.** The working tree is 105 files ahead of HEAD: the other session's eight hub
   pages, new dispatch, JSON-LD and breadcrumb expansion and feed rewiring, plus this run's fixes.
   None of it is live. The push is left for a supervised run for two reasons: this session cannot
   delete files in the folder, which makes git lock-file handling unsafe unattended, and the other
   scheduled session may not have finished its own work.
   `git add -A && git commit -m "hub pages + QA 10-12" && git push`
3. The two orphan draft pages that have been a standing item since August are **resolved**: the other
   session converted both to noindex redirect stubs pointing at
   `how-to-automatically-follow-up-on-unpaid-draft-orders-on-shopify.html`. The tree now has no
   duplicate dispatch numbers and no titles past 60 characters.
4. Micro-labels (tag pills at 10.24 px, card dates and byline avatars at ~11 px, footer headings and
   the ticker at 11.52 px) still render under 12 px at 768 px and wider. This is deliberate editorial
   typography and the 12 px floor is a mobile legibility rule, so desktop was left alone; it is noted
   here only so the next run does not rediscover it as a defect.
5. The homepage ticker is an infinite marquee with no pause control. It is `aria-hidden="true"` and
   the tree honors `prefers-reduced-motion`, so this was left as is.
6. Inline links inside body copy render 21 px tall at phone widths. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 exempts targets
   constrained by the line-height of surrounding text.
7. `_qa_tmp/` holds this run's before and after tarballs and the md5 manifest.
