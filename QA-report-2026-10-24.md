# QA report, 2026-10-24

Scope: every rendered page on blog.dugong.live, checked for formatting, design,
mobile friendliness, readability and content accuracy. Pages were rendered in
headless Chromium at 320, 360, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS pixels with the real
Hanken Grotesk, Inter and JetBrains Mono webfonts loaded, not fallbacks.

Note on timing: this pass started against 100 rendered pages and 87 dispatches.
Partway through, two other runs fired into the repository at once: an SEO/GEO
pass that added a `.short-answer` invariant to `_tools/seo-check.py` and wrapped
short answers across 26 pages, and the daily publishing run, which shipped
dispatch Nº 134 (`how-to-find-shopify-products-missing-images-or-weights.html`).
Editing stopped, the repository was polled until it had been quiet for four
minutes, and then the whole pass was redone from a fresh snapshot: every check
re-run, every finding re-confirmed against the new state, and the whole site
re-rendered at all five widths. Final count: 101 rendered pages, 88 dispatches.

Two findings did not survive that redo and were dropped rather than reported:
the orphaned Nº 133 dispatch picked up an editorial inbound link from the
concurrent run, and one of the two inbound-link gaps closed itself the same way.
One new finding arrived with Nº 134 (`judgement`, item 6).

`_tools/seo-check.py` passed before this pass and passes after it, against the
stricter version the concurrent run left behind.

## What was wrong, and what was done

### 1. The Nº 132 dispatch contradicted itself in its first line

The ticker at the top of
`how-to-stop-shipping-shopify-orders-with-payment-pending.html` read
`DISPATCH Nº 131 · OCT 18, 2026`, twice, because the marquee repeats its own
content to loop. The page's masthead, its `article:published_time`, its sitemap
entry and its JSON-LD all say Nº 132, OCT 19. The ticker had been copied from
the previous day's dispatch and never updated.

This was the only wrong dispatch number on the site. Read through the ticker,
the sequence looked like it had a duplicate Nº 131 and a missing Nº 132; read
through the mastheads it is clean. With the ticker corrected, dispatch numbers
run 47 to 134 across 88 dispatches with no duplicate, no gap, and strictly
increasing by publication date. That also settles the question the last three
passes flagged about `134 dispatches published` on the homepage: the number is
the issue number, and the sequence behind it is now intact and checkable.

### 2. The homepage undercounted the question index by twelve

index.html said the question index "lists all 433 questions". The index lists
445, which is what llms.txt and the index page's own masthead already said. The
daily run updates the index page and llms.txt and does not touch this sentence,
so the number had been drifting since at least 10-21.

### 3. Forty-three dispatches had no visible way up to their topic guide

Every dispatch page declares a `BreadcrumbList` in its JSON-LD placing it under
one of the eight topic guides, and every dispatch is listed by exactly one guide.
But only 45 of 88 carried the `.hub-more` link that is the site's one visible
route up that hierarchy. On the other 43 a reader finishing a dispatch could go
sideways to six related dispatches or out to the question index, and had no way
to reach the guide the page belongs to.

All 43 now carry it, pointing at the guide that actually lists them, in the
existing markup and lowercase microcopy: `more on shopify inventory automation →`.
The `.hub-more` CSS, including the 24px tap-target rule the 2026-10-12 pass
added, was already on every page, so nothing in the stylesheet changed. 87 of 88
dispatches now have the link; the lead essay belongs to no guide and is the
documented exception.

### 4. Four copy defects

| Page | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| payment-pending dispatch | "a human pressing fulfil anyway" | `fulfill`, matching Shopify's button and `fulfillment` four words earlier in the same sentence |
| payment-pending dispatch, dek | "picked, packed, labelled and shipped" | `labeled`, against 27 files using the one-l form and no other prose instance of the other |
| Flow-failures dispatch | "Canceled means the workflow was cancelled before it could complete" | "...was stopped...". Flow's status name `Canceled` is quoted verbatim and stays; only the editorial half changed, so the sentence no longer carries both spellings |
| Nº 134, published today | "every judgement involved is a rule" | `judgment`, against 152 files using the one-e form. Fixed in the visible FAQ answer and the JSON-LD copy of it together, so they still match |

`cancelled` was left as written in the other 114 places: it is the established
term across the site, it matches Shopify's own `cancelledAt` API field, and one
of the instances is a dispatch headline that is also a slug, a canonical URL and
a sitemap entry. The six `canceled` instances were left too, each quoting a
Shopify UI status or Flow trigger name verbatim. `Personalisation` was left as
written: it appears only inside the quoted title of a Shopify community thread.

### 5. One footer link now has a real destination

The footer points seven differently labelled links at `https://dugong.live`.
Fetching that site's own navigation settles one of them: it has a `#pricing`
section, so `Pricing` now goes to `https://dugong.live/#pricing` on all 100
pages that carry the footer.

The other six are still pointed at the bare homepage, and this pass could not
fix them honestly. dugong.live is a single page; it has no About, Careers, Docs
or Privacy page to link, and no separate AI Engine page. A reader clicking
`Privacy` lands on a marketing homepage. Guessing a destination would be worse
than the current state, and removing the labels is a decision this pass should
not make. See below.

## What was checked and found clean

* No horizontal scroll, no element past the viewport, no clipped text on any of
  101 pages at 320, 360, 390, 768 or 1280px.
* No text below 12px at any width up to and including 1024px.
* Contrast: zero failures against WCAG 1.4.3 on any non-decorative text, at all
  five widths. The checker composites translucent backgrounds over their
  parents; checked naively it reports a false failure on `.tag-essay`, which
  actually runs at 5.39:1.
* Tap targets: zero under 24px, applying the WCAG 2.5.8 inline-link exemption.
* No console error, no page error, no broken image (the site uses no `<img>`).
* All 101 pages parse under a strict HTML5 parser; one `h1` per page, no
  heading-level skips, no duplicate element ids.
* No em or en dashes, no curly quotes, no mojibake, no raw entities in visible
  copy, no double spaces, no unbalanced quotes or parentheses, no placeholder
  text.
* Dispatch numbering: 47 to 134, no duplicates, no gaps, strictly increasing by
  publication date.
* Counts and date ranges on all eight topic guides, both author pages and the
  question index match the dispatches each page actually lists. This is the
  first pass in five that found none of them stale.
* READ NEXT convention holds: six visible cards and the remainder behind a
  `<details>` on every archive-shaped grid, and the shorter curated grids on
  recent dispatches carry six or fewer, so they need no disclosure.
* Every dispatch has at least one editorial inbound link.
* Word-count claims in the tickers sit within 13% of a recount on all 88
  dispatches, median ratio 0.99; read times imply 199 to 312 words per minute.
* `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured on every animated page.
* Page height at 390px: median 21,772px, worst 32,634px (priya-singh.html, the
  full author index).

## Known, deliberate, left alone

* Micro-label typography above 1024px: topic tags at 10.24px, card dates and
  authors at 11.2px, read-time labels at 11.52px. The 2026-10-21 pass raised the
  12px legibility floor to 1024px and documented desktop as intentionally
  unchanged. There is no WCAG minimum font size and this is a deliberate
  editorial design, so it stands, but it is the only readability item on the
  site a reasonable person would still argue about.
* The byline and hero separator dots run at 3.03:1 and are `aria-hidden="true"`.
  Decorative glyphs, correctly marked; 1.4.3 does not apply.
* One FAQ answer begins a sentence with the lowercase GraphQL mutation name
  `metafieldsSet`. Capitalising it would be wrong, since the name is
  case-sensitive, and rewording accurate technical copy to satisfy a prose
  convention is not worth the risk of changing its meaning.

## Needs a decision, not a fix

The footer's About, Careers, Docs, Privacy and AI Engine links. All five point
at `https://dugong.live`, which has no such pages. Either those pages get built
and the links get pointed at them, or the labels come out of the footer. The
third pass in a row to raise this; `Privacy` in particular is a label most
readers expect to resolve.
