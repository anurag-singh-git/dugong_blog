# QA report - 2026-10-17

Scheduled autonomous run. Full-tree QA of blog.dugong.live.

Today's blog run finished at 09:25 UTC with Dispatch Nº 130,
`how-to-stop-shopify-b2b-prices-dropping-when-you-run-a-sale.html`, and today's SEO/GEO run was
still writing when this audit opened. Rather than audit a moving tree, the read-only passes ran
first and every write waited until the tree had gone quiet: the SEO run's last edit landed at
09:48, its report at ~10:05, and all fixes below were applied after that against a verified-stable
tree. The tree settled at 178 HTML files, 95 rendered (84 dispatches including the lead essay, 8
topic hubs, the question index, the author page, index.html) plus 404.html and 82 redirect stubs,
and it has not moved since.

Twenty-one defects were found and fixed, in three groups. Every edit was a read-modify-write
against the live file, guarded by an exact-occurrence-count assertion and a byte-delta assertion
that abort the whole patch rather than write a near-miss. No facts, headlines, body structure or
visible copy were changed: the FAQ fix below asserts the visible answers are byte-identical before
and after.

**New this run: FAQ answer parity between the JSON-LD and the visible copy.** Earlier runs checked
that every FAQPage *question* appears in the visible copy; none had checked the *answers*. Fifteen
were different drafts. That is defect 3 and it is the most substantive thing this audit found.

**Also new: the render harness now serves the tree over HTTP rather than `file://`.** That resolves
the root-absolute asset paths (`/favicon.svg`, `/feed.xml`) exactly as the live domain does, and it
clears standing item 2: across 1,394 renders this run there were **zero** console errors, zero page
errors and zero failed requests. The two errors every earlier run logged were both artifacts of
loading the tree off disk behind a proxy, not site faults.

**And: the contrast pass now applies group opacity the way the compositor does.** An ancestor with
`opacity < 1` fades its own background as well as its text, so both must be composited over what
sits behind before the ratio is taken. Fading only the text (which is what measuring the text alpha
against a full-strength ancestor background amounts to) understates some ratios and overstates
others. With the correct model, the tree has exactly two failing text nodes, and they are a real
pre-existing shortfall that earlier runs reported as clean. See new standing item 13.

## Defects found and fixed

   - `how-to-handle-price-adjustment-requests-on-shopify.html`, 3, same FAQ duplication.

   - `how-to-handle-shopify-returns-the-customer-never-sends-back.html`, 2 x `Ageing`, both

   in llms.txt. All nine in-page occurrences plus the five md renditions, llms.txt, llms-full.txt
   and feed.xml were changed together, and feed.xml and sitemap.xml were re-parsed as well-formed

3. **Fifteen FAQ answers carried one draft in the visible copy and a different, older draft in the
   FAQPage JSON-LD.** Three pages, every one of their five FAQs: the 10-10 wholesale-tax-exempt
   post, the 10-10 price-adjustment-requests post and the 10-11 returns-never-sent-back post. These
   are not typos, they are whole paraphrases, which is how they survived four months of audits that
   only checked whether the *question* appeared in the visible copy. Examples:
   - JSON-LD: "Shopify stores the exemption as a flag on the customer, taxExempt, plus an optional
     list of per-state exemptions such as `US_CA_RESELLER_EXEMPTION` or `US_FL_RESELLER_EXEMPTION`."
     Page: the same sentence listing `US_CA_RESELLER_EXEMPTION` only.
   - JSON-LD: "The exemption covers the whole customer rather than specific products, so it also
     removes tax from anything else they buy". Page: the same sentence without that final clause.
   - JSON-LD: "Refunding on approval is the single decision that converts a quiet return..."
     Page: "...that turns a quiet return...".
   - The page's closing advice "Confirm each rule with the relevant state before you encode it" was
     absent from the JSON-LD entirely, which instead ended on a sentence about "taxed states billed
     as exempt" that appears nowhere on the page.

   Two things were wrong with this. Google's structured-data guidance requires FAQPage content to be
   present on the page, so fifteen answers that are not were a policy risk on three pages that are
   deliberately built to be cited by answer engines. And the structured data was asserting things
   the article does not say, which is worse than a formatting slip: `US_FL_RESELLER_EXEMPTION` as a
   per-state exemption code, and a claim about what a customer-level exemption covers.

   The visible copy is what readers see and the tighter of the two drafts, so it was treated as the
   source of truth and the JSON-LD was resynced to it. Only `acceptedAnswer.text` string literals

   JSON-LD, re-checked every answer against its visible paragraph, and **asserted that the serialised
   `.faq-a` elements were byte-identical before and after**, so no reader-facing copy moved.

   The same stale drafts had also reached llms-full.txt, whose per-article FAQ sections for those
   three posts carried the JSON-LD wording in 10 lines. Those were resynced to the visible copy too.
   The `md/` renditions were already correct, having been generated from the visible copy.
   **All 421 FAQ answers in the tree now agree across the page, the JSON-LD and llms-full.txt.**

## Content verification

Nº 130's central claim was checked against the primary source rather than taken from the blog run's
notes. The Help Center page *Customizing B2B pricing using catalogs* states that "fixed prices
override any overall adjustments that you set", and works the post's own example: candles at ten
dollars, an overall adjustment of -20% taking them to eight for companies with catalog access, and
a nine dollar fixed price on one product that stays at nine while the rest of the catalog drops.
The plan caps the post gives are the documented ones, 3 active catalogs on Basic, Grow and
Advanced, a maximum of 25 catalogs per company location and 10,000 per store, and the documented
rule that the lowest price displays where several catalogs assigned to the same location carry the
same product. The post's CSV claim, that exported catalog files include only fixed-price products
and not the percentage-adjusted prices, is the documented behaviour and is stated accurately. The
post does not repeat the one place where the blog run's notes were looser than the source: the
first-created-catalog tie-break in the documentation governs which catalog's quantity rules and
volume pricing display, not which price wins, and the post makes no tie-break claim at all. The
thesis, that a B2B catalog sits in the pricing system and is computed from the product's standard
store price so every markdown moves the trade floor, is what the source describes.

    its text, so `--text-faint` #71716a composites to about rgb(153,153,148) over an effective
    rgb(252,252,249), and the sub-label "fallback -> #ops alert" measures **2.83:1** where AA needs

    lighter background and a full-strength text colour rather than a blanket opacity on the
