this run: the day's publishing run shipped Nº 141 at 19:07 UTC while the
audit was in progress, taking the tree from 107 rendered pages to 108 and
from 94 dispatches to 95. Everything below was re-run against the final
state, and all counts are the final ones: 108 rendered pages, 82 redirect

## Fixed

1. **Every fragment link landed underneath the sticky nav.** `.nav` is
   `position: sticky; top: 0`, 66px tall above 820px and 89 to 93px below it
   where the row wraps onto two lines. Not one page declared
   `scroll-padding-top`, so the browser scrolled each anchor target to y=0 and
   the nav then covered its first ~92px. Measured before the fix:
   `#faq-1` on a dispatch and `#main` on another both settled with the
   target's top edge at y=0 under a bar whose bottom edge was at y=92. That
   is the `#main` skip link on 107 pages, which is the one control on the
   page whose whole purpose is to land the keyboard user on the content, plus
   the six `index.html#...` links in every page's footer and 470
   `...#faq-N` links. WCAG 2.4.1. Added to all 108 pages:

   ```css
   html { scroll-behavior: smooth; scroll-padding-top: 105px; }
   @media (min-width: 821px) { html { scroll-padding-top: 78px; } }
   ```

   Re-measured in a browser: `#faq-1`, `#main`, `#archive` and `#latest` now
   settle at y=105 on a 375px screen against a nav bottom of 92, and at y=78
   on a 1280px screen against a nav bottom of 66. 404.html is the one page
   with no nav and is left alone, by the same condition that exempts it from
   carrying a skip link.

2. **FROM THE ARCHIVE was six rows of template filler.** The "Archive" link
   in the primary nav of all 108 pages lands on `#archive`. It held six
   `<li>` elements numbered 036 to 041, dated MAR 24 to APR 28, titled "A
   short history of if-this-then-that", "Composable triggers: a half-baked
   manifesto", "Why your ops team is secretly a compiler", "The case for
   boring workflows", "Counting the hidden cost of a single Zap" and "When
   automation stops being a tool and starts being a teammate". None of those
   six strings appears anywhere else in the repository, in feed.xml, in
   llms.txt, in llms-full.txt or in `/md/`. They describe six articles that
   were never published, and the rows carried no anchor at all: six dead
   lines, one click from every page on the site. The weekly cadence they
   imply does not join up with the real series either, which starts at Nº 47
   on 2026-05-28. Replaced with the six oldest real dispatches, Nº 47 to 52,
   each linked, each with its own number and publication date. The section's
   own "browse the recent dispatches" call to action now makes sense: the
   archive holds the oldest, the grid above holds the newest.

3. **DISPATCH OF THE WEEK had not moved in 48 dispatches.**
   `<section class="featured" id="latest">` is where the "Latest" nav link on
   every other page points, and it still linked Nº 47, "The death of the
   drag-and-drop builder", published 2026-05-28. The hero one screen above it
   read "ISSUE Nº 141 / NOV 16 / 2026 / LATEST DISPATCH". The publishing run
   writes a card, a ticker count, a tag-cloud entry and a `blogPost` node on
   publication day and has never touched this block, so the site's flagship
   slot could only drift further out of date. Rotated to Nº 141, "How to fix
   Shopify orders with no phone number before the label fails", with its tag,
   reading time, dek and byline taken from the page itself, and the playbook
   mock rewritten from that dispatch's own `<pre id="playbook">` steps rather
   than invented. Checked at 375px and 1280px: nothing wraps badly, the
   branch arms fit, and the card reads as it did before.

4. **The new archive links were 21px tall.** Caught by re-running the tap
   target sweep after fix 2, which is the reason it is in this list: the fix
   introduced it. WCAG 2.5.8 asks for 24px. The anchors now carry
   `display: inline-block` with vertical padding, taking them to 29px at
   desktop and 52 to 76px on a phone, and a stretched `::after` makes the
   whole 73px row the hit area, which is what the existing
   `li[data-nav]:hover` styling always implied. Verified by clicking 12px
   from the right edge of the first row at both 375px and 1280px: both land
   on the dispatch.

## Added to the checker

Three new invariants in `_tools/seo-check.py`, each with its row in
`_tools/README.md`, each tested in both directions before being kept:

* **no fragment target lands underneath the sticky nav.** Every rendered page
  that has a sticky nav must declare a base `scroll-padding-top` and a
  >820px override. This is item 1 stated as an invariant.
* **the homepage's DISPATCH OF THE WEEK links the newest dispatch**, which is
  item 3. Ties on the newest publication date are all accepted. This is the
  durable half of that fix: rotating the card once only helps until the next
  publishing run, and the run will now fail its own gate if it ships a
  dispatch without moving the slot.
* **every row under FROM THE ARCHIVE links a page that exists**, which is
  item 2. It catches both a row with no anchor and a row pointing at a file
  that is not there.

## Open, not actioned

* **The newsletter form does not subscribe anyone.** The signup block on
  index.html is a `<form>` whose entire behavior is
  `onsubmit="event.preventDefault(); button.textContent='subscribed ✓';
  input.value=''"`. There is no endpoint, no request and no storage. A reader
  types their address, is told "subscribed ✓" next to the words "free ·
  unsubscribe anytime · 4,200 readers", and the address is discarded. This is
  the one defect in this report that tells the reader something untrue, and
  it is the one this pass deliberately did not touch: every honest fix picks
  an email provider or an endpoint, and that is an infrastructure decision
  rather than a QA one. Three shapes it could take: wire the form to a real
  list, point it at the `field-notes@dugong.live` inbox the dispatches
  already publish, or remove the block until there is a list behind it.
* **The live site is behind the repository by 11 dispatches and 17 days.**
  The last commit is `1059055` on 2026-10-30. 177 paths are uncommitted,
  including five dispatch pages that have never existed outside this machine,
  Nos 137 to 141. Committing and pushing publishes content, which is a
  decision for a person and not for a QA run, so nothing here changes it.
  Everything in this report is about files that readers cannot currently see.
* **The homepage puts `banner`, `navigation` and `contentinfo` inside
  `role="main"`.** Carried over unchanged from 2026-11-04. `#view-home`
  carries the main role and wraps the ticker, the header, the nav and the
  footer. Assistive technology still exposes all four landmarks, so the
  practical cost is small, and the fix is not a one-attribute change:
  `.view.active.home > .hero > *` drives the hero entrance animation and the
  reveal script reads `.view.active .foot`.
* **24 paragraphs run past 170 words and 85 sentences past 70.** Editorial
  judgment, not a defect. The corpus averages 20.1 words per sentence with a
  median of 17, which is comfortable; the tail is what is long.
* **External link liveness is unverified.** Outbound HTTP to arbitrary hosts
  is refused by the egress proxy in both shells available to this run, so
  none of the 236 distinct external URLs could be fetched.
* **Layout was verified under the fallback font stack.** fonts.googleapis.com
  is blocked from both shells for the same reason, so every browser
  measurement above was taken with `system-ui` standing in for Hanken
  Grotesk, Inter and JetBrains Mono. That is a real reading of the site,
  since it is what a reader with the fonts blocked or slow sees, but it is
  not a reading of the typeface the house ships. Worth one pass from a
  machine with outbound access.
