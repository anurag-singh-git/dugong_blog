# QA report, 2026-10-31

Scope: every rendered page on blog.dugong.live, checked for formatting, design,
mobile friendliness, readability and content. 104 rendered pages, 82 redirect
stubs, 91 dispatches, numbered 47 to 137. Pages were rendered in headless
Chromium at 320, 360, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS pixels.

Note on timing: this pass began at 20:34 UTC against a tree whose last commit
was 2026-10-30. The day's publishing run fired at about 20:46 while it was in
progress and ran until 20:53, shipping dispatch Nº 137
(`how-to-handle-wrong-item-shipped-claims-on-shopify.html`), backfilling the
short answer into the last 21 dispatches that lacked one, and adding a check of
its own to `seo-check.py`. The first fix of this pass landed at 20:46:39, in the
middle of that. Nothing was lost in either direction: the run edits pages in
place rather than rewriting them from a template, so every page it touched after
20:46 still carries the fix, and the fix was applied to the 21 pages the run had
just given the block to. The repository was then polled until it had been quiet
for more than five minutes, and the whole verification below, including the
render pass, was re-run from scratch against that settled tree with Nº 137
included. The commit this report accompanies therefore carries the publishing
run's output as well as this pass's fixes.

Note on fonts: the webfont hosts are not reachable from either shell, so Hanken
Grotesk, Inter and JetBrains Mono were installed from their npm packages and
served to the renderer in place of the Google Fonts request. The measured
glyphs are the real ones, which is what the overflow and small-text numbers
depend on.

`_tools/seo-check.py` passed before this pass and passes after it.

## What was wrong, and what was done

### 1. The short answer was a styled block that was never styled

`class="short-answer"` is on 91 dispatches. It was introduced on 2026-10-24 and
extended on 10-27 and again on 10-31, and in all that time no page ever defined
a CSS rule for it. The container computed to nothing at all: no background, no
border, no padding, no margin, no radius. Its paragraphs computed identically to
any other paragraph in the article body, 19.2px at line-height 34.56px in
`var(--text)`, and its `h2` computed identically to every other section heading
at 28.8px. Read as a picture of the page, the one passage written to be lifted
whole as the answer was indistinguishable from the prose around it.

What makes it worth fixing rather than noting is the asymmetry it left. The
JSON-LD names `.short-answer` in `speakable`, carries the paragraph in
`BlogPosting.abstract`, and the block has its own `id="short-answer"` for
fragment citation. llms.txt, llms-full.txt and the md renditions all carry it as
a marked section. Every machine that reads the page had been told the block was
special. The reader was the only party that had not.

This is the same failure shape as the `.article-dek` anchor defect found on
2026-10-21, markup shipped ahead of the stylesheet, and the fix follows the
house vocabulary for marked blocks rather than inventing one. `.callout` is a
tinted panel with a 12px radius and a 3px `--lime-deep` left rule; `blockquote`
is a 4px `--lime` left rule. The short answer now reads as a white panel lifted
off the `#f6f6f1` page, with the same 12px radius and the same 3px `--lime-deep`
left rule, and its `h2` becomes a small uppercase eyebrow in `--lime-deep`
rather than a section heading competing with the real ones below it:

```
.short-answer { background: var(--surface); border: 1px solid var(--border);
  border-left: 3px solid var(--lime-deep); border-radius: 12px;
  padding: 1.5rem 1.75rem; margin: 2.25rem 0 2.75rem; }
.article-body .short-answer h2 { font-family: var(--display); font-weight: 700;
  font-size: 0.8rem; line-height: 1.2; letter-spacing: 0.1em;
  text-transform: uppercase; color: var(--lime-deep); margin: 0 0 1rem; }
.article-body .short-answer p:last-child { margin-bottom: 0; }
```

with a padding and radius reduction inside the existing `max-width: 1024px`
block, next to the `.callout` override already there.

The body text keeps its reading size. The block is three to six paragraphs of
substance, not an aside, and enlarging it would have made the page shout. The
eyebrow sits at 0.8rem, which is 12.8px, above the 0.64rem chip floor and
needing no mobile bump of its own. Its `--lime-deep` on white is 5.75:1, inside
AA for normal text. The panel itself is only 1.08:1 against the page, which is
the intended effect: the left rule and the radius carry the boundary, not a
colour change that would fight the lime accent.

Verified on all 91 pages at 1280 and 360: the computed container resolves to
exactly two shapes, 24px/28px padding at a 12px radius on desktop and
19.2px/20px at a 10px radius below 1024, with the left rule, the uppercase
eyebrow and the zeroed last-paragraph margin identical on every one, and no page
overflowing its viewport.

### 2. The homepage hero masthead was an issue and a week behind

`index.html` opens with three claims about the newest dispatch: `.hero-issue`
read `ISSUE Nº 136`, `.hero-date` read `OCT 24 · 2026`, and the first
`.stat-num` read `136`. Nº 137 had shipped that morning and is dated 2026-10-31,
so all three were wrong, under a `LATEST DISPATCH` label that asserts they are
current.

The date had already been wrong before today. Nº 136 published on 2026-10-25,
and the run that shipped it advanced the issue number and left the date on
OCT 24, so the masthead had been misdating the newest dispatch for six days. The
hero is written at publication and no later run goes back over it, which is the
same drift shape as the READ NEXT grids found on 10-21 and the tag cloud found
on 10-25.

`.stat-num` was read against its own history rather than reinterpreted. It has
equalled the hero issue number in every commit that touched the hero, 136/136,
135/135, 133/133 and back to 118/118, so it tracks the issue series and not the
count of pages, and it was advanced with the series rather than rewritten to the
91 dispatch pages the site actually holds. That would have been an editorial
decision about what the blog claims, not a QA fix. All three now read 137,
OCT 31 · 2026, and 137.

## Two invariants added

Both defects above are invisible to a checker that reads fields, which is why
neither was caught by the thirty-odd invariants already in `seo-check.py`. Both
are now checked, and both were confirmed to fire on the defect and pass once it
is fixed, by reintroducing each defect and running the checker against it.

**Every class used in a page's markup has at least one rule in that page's
stylesheet.** This is the generalisable form of both the `.short-answer` defect
and the `.article-dek` defect before it: markup shipped ahead of the stylesheet.
Four classes are exempted in `EXCEPTIONS["unstyled_class"]`, `faq-list`,
`signup-left`, `hero-date` and `qa-post`. The first three and the last are bare
grouping wrappers or a label that inherits its parent's type on purpose, and
each was confirmed to render identically with and without a rule.

**The homepage hero names the newest dispatch.** The check reads the newest
dispatch the site actually has, by published date and then issue number, and
requires `.hero-issue`, the first `.stat-num` and `.hero-date` to agree with it.

Both are documented in `_tools/README.md` alongside the rest.
