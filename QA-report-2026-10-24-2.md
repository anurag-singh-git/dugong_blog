# QA report, 2026-10-24 (second run)

Scope: every rendered page on blog.dugong.live, checked for formatting, design,
mobile friendliness, readability and content accuracy. 102 rendered pages, 89
dispatches, numbered 47 to 135. Pages were rendered in headless Chromium at
320, 360, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS pixels.

Note on timing: this run fired at 09:41 UTC while the day's second publishing
run was still writing into the repository, three minutes after it had last
touched a file. Editing was held off and the repository polled until it had
been quiet for four minutes, which it reached at 09:56, after that run shipped
dispatch Nº 135 and the second SEO/GEO pass finished. Every check below ran
against that settled state, and the repository stayed quiet throughout.

Note on fonts: the webfont hosts are not reachable from either shell today, so
Hanken Grotesk, Inter and JetBrains Mono were installed from their npm
packages and served to the renderer in place of the Google Fonts request. The
measured glyphs are therefore the real ones, not fallback metrics, which is
what the overflow and small-text numbers below depend on.

`_tools/seo-check.py` passed before this pass and passes after it.

## What was wrong, and what was done

### 1. The inventory guide contradicted itself twice about its own length

`shopify-inventory-automation.html` lists 18 dispatches. It has 18 cards, its
JSON-LD `ItemList` holds 18 entries, and llms.txt says 18. Its masthead said
`17 DISPATCHES` and the heading over the list itself said
`3 DISPATCHES IN THIS GUIDE`.

The publishing run that shipped Nº 135 added the dispatch to the card grid, the
ItemList and llms.txt, and left both hand-written counts behind. The `3` is the
worse of the two: a reader arriving at the guide that covers the largest topic
on the site was told it held three articles above a grid of eighteen. Both now
read 18. The other seven guides were checked the same way and all agree with
themselves.

### 2. The question index undercounted its own dispatches

`shopify-automation-questions.html` said `451 QUESTIONS · 87 DISPATCHES`. The
eight guides list 18+17+18+8+7+7+8+5 = 88, and llms.txt already said 88. Now 88.

### 3. The homepage undercounted the question index again, by six

index.html said the question index "lists all 445 questions". It lists 451.
The 02:48 pass the same day corrected this sentence from 433 to 445; two
dispatches shipped afterwards and it drifted again within hours. The daily run
updates the index page and llms.txt and never touches this sentence, so it will
keep drifting; it is now covered by a check (see below).

### 4. Both dispatches published today claimed to be shorter reads than they are

Across the other 87 dispatches, claimed words divided by claimed minutes runs
172 to 312, median 263. The two shipped today were both labelled 10 min:

| Dispatch | Words | Was | Claimed words per minute | Now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nº 135, inventory transfers | 3,560 | 10 min | 356, the highest on the site | 13 min (274) |
| Nº 134, products missing images or weights | 3,258 | 10 min | 326 | 12 min (272) |

13 and 12 minutes follow the site's own precedent: the two longest previous
dispatches, at 2,900 and 3,000 words, are labelled 11 min, which is 264 and 273
words per minute. The figure appears in five places per dispatch and on every
card that links it, so each was changed in all of them together: the ticker
twice, the masthead, the JSON-LD `timeRequired`, the Markdown alternate, and 77
cards across 77 pages. The band now runs 172 to 300.

### 5. feed.xml was published with timestamps in the future

`lastBuildDate` read 13:30 UTC and the two newest items were dated 13:30 and
12:00, against a real time of 10:14. Aggregators commonly suppress or mis-sort
a future-dated item, and a feed asserting it was built three hours from now is
simply untrue. The two items moved to 10:00 and 09:00, times the feed already
uses elsewhere, which keeps the newest-first order intact, and `lastBuildDate`
became the real build time. Every one of the 89 item dates is now in the past.

### 6. One house-style slip

`how-to-handle-return-to-sender-orders-on-shopify.html` used `travelled` twice
against a single `traveled` elsewhere on the site, and the site uses the
American one-l form throughout: `labeled`, `fulfill`, `judgment`,
`personalization`. Corrected in the page, its Markdown alternate, llms.txt and
llms-full.txt, so the four renditions still match. `cancelled` was left as
written in all 258 places for the reasons the 10-24 pass recorded, and
`Personalisation` was left alone: it appears only inside the quoted title of a
Shopify community thread.

## Checks added to `_tools/seo-check.py`

Three of the six findings above are numbers written by hand next to a list the
generator already knows the length of, which is why they drift. All are now
invariants, and each was confirmed to fire against the pre-fix state:

* every count a page writes next to a list it owns agrees with that list: each
  guide's masthead and list heading against its ItemList and card count, the
  question index masthead against the eight guides, the homepage's question
  count against the index.
* claimed words divided by claimed minutes falls between 150 and 320.
* no `pubDate` or `lastBuildDate` in feed.xml is in the future.

Run against the state this pass started from, they report exactly the nine
defects fixed above and nothing else. `_tools/README.md` carries the reasons.

## What was checked and found clean

* No horizontal scroll and no element past the viewport on any of 102 pages at
  320, 360, 390, 768 or 1280px.
* No clipped text anywhere. The card deks that look truncated are
  `-webkit-line-clamp` doing it deliberately, with an ellipsis.
* Contrast: zero failures against WCAG 1.4.3 on any non-decorative text at any
  of the five widths, compositing translucent backgrounds over their parents.
* Tap targets: zero under 24px, applying the WCAG 2.5.8 inline-link exemption.
* No text below 12px at any width up to and including 1024px.
* No console error, no page error, no load failure, no broken image.
* One `h1` per page, no heading-level skips, no duplicate element ids, `lang`
  set on every page, every `<title>` within 60 characters.
* No `<pre>` or table overflows its container unscrollably at 320px. The
  playbook blocks scroll horizontally by design.
* The reveal animation was checked under a real reader's scroll at 390px: every
  `.reveal` element ends up fully opaque, and the one in-page anchor leaves
  nothing invisible. The class is added by JavaScript, so with scripting off
  the article renders at full opacity rather than disappearing.
* `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured on every animated page.
* Dispatch numbering: 47 to 135 across 89 dispatches, no duplicate, no gap,
  strictly increasing by publication date.
* Every dispatch's ticker agrees with its masthead, its
  `article:published_time` and its sitemap entry on both number and date.
* Word-count claims sit within 13% of a recount on all 89 dispatches, median
  ratio 1.00.
* All 89 dispatches carry the `.hub-more` route up to their topic guide, and
  both dispatches published today have an editorial inbound link already.
* No em or en dashes, no curly quotes, no mojibake, no raw entities in visible
  copy, no double spaces, no placeholder text, no duplicated words. Two
  apparent duplicates were read in context and are correct English: "double
  opt-in in particular" and "what a customer is is not a slot to fill".
* No page metadata carries a future date.
* Page height at 390px: median 22,197px, worst 33,340px (priya-singh.html, the
  full author index).

## Known, deliberate, left alone

* Micro-label typography above 1024px: topic tags at 10.24px, card dates and
  authors at 11.2px, read-time labels at 11.52px. Documented as intentional by
  the 2026-10-21 pass; there is no WCAG minimum font size. Still the only
  readability item on the site a reasonable person would argue about.
* The byline and hero separator dots run at 3.03:1 and are `aria-hidden="true"`.
* One FAQ answer opens a sentence with the lowercase mutation name
  `metafieldsSet`, which is case-sensitive and should not be capitalised.
* 48 of the 89 dispatches carry no short answer. The 41 that do are exactly the
  41 declared in llms-full.txt, so nothing is inconsistent; the block is being
  rolled out and the remaining 48 need written answers, not a mechanical fix.

## Needs a decision, not a fix

The footer's About, Careers, Docs, Privacy and AI Engine links. All five point
at `https://dugong.live`, which has no such pages. Either those pages get built
and the links get pointed at them, or the labels come out of the footer. The
fourth pass in a row to raise this. `Privacy` in particular is a label most
readers expect to resolve, and a privacy link that lands on a marketing
homepage is the kind of thing a reader reads as evasive rather than unfinished.
