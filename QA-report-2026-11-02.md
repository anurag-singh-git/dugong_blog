# QA report, 2026-11-02

Scope: every rendered page on blog.dugong.live, checked for formatting, design,
mobile friendliness, readability and content. 105 rendered pages, 82 redirect
stubs, 92 dispatches, numbered 47 to 138. Pages were rendered in headless
Chromium at 320, 360, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS pixels, 525 renders in all.

Note on timing: this pass began at 19:23 UTC against a tree whose newest
dispatch was Nº 137. The day's publishing run fired at about 19:37 while the
first audit was still running and shipped dispatch Nº 138,
`how-to-find-shopify-products-with-no-cost-per-item.html`, dated 2026-11-02. It
also advanced the hero masthead to Nº 138 and NOV 2, recomputed the ticker's
month count, and added a section-heading `id` invariant of its own. The
repository was then polled until it had been quiet for more than five minutes,
and every finding below was re-verified from scratch against that settled tree
before anything was changed. The first fix landed at 19:46, after the run was
done, so nothing was written across it in either direction.

Note on fonts: the webfont hosts are not reachable from either shell, so Hanken
Grotesk, Inter and JetBrains Mono were installed from their npm packages and
served to the renderer in place of the Google Fonts request. The measured
glyphs are the real ones, which is what the overflow, small-text and contrast
numbers depend on.

`_tools/seo-check.py` passed before this pass and passes after it.

## What was wrong, and what was done

### 1. 404.html was missing the newest dispatch but one

`404.html` carries a *Latest dispatches* list of the ten newest dispatches. It
is the only index that page offers, and it is the whole reason the page is more
than an apology. On the morning of this pass the list ran Nº 137's predecessor
first and Nº 137 was absent. By the time the day's run had finished it ran
Nº 138, then jumped straight to 2026-10-25, with 2026-10-31 missing from the
middle.

The mechanism is worth stating because it makes the defect permanent rather
than late. The run prepends the dispatch it has just shipped and drops the
oldest to hold the list at ten. A dispatch the run never added is therefore not
behind the window, it is outside it, and every later run pushes it further out.
Nº 137 shipped on 2026-10-31 and was never added, so the run that shipped
Nº 138 two days later did not repair the gap, it sealed it. Left alone, Nº 137
would never have appeared on that page at all.

This is the same drift shape as the hero masthead found on 10-31, the READ NEXT
grids found on 10-21 and the tag cloud found on 10-25: a surface written at
publication that no later run goes back over. `how-to-handle-wrong-item-shipped-claims-on-shopify.html`
was inserted in second place as
*How to handle wrong item shipped claims on Shopify*, and the oldest entry,
*Stop reordering Shopify stock already on order*, was dropped to keep the list
at ten. The list now holds exactly the ten newest dispatches, newest first, and
no entry carries the ` | Dugong` suffix the 10-25 check forbids.

### 2. 404.html was the one page with an unbranded focus ring

104 of the 105 rendered pages define

```
a:focus-visible, button:focus-visible, input:focus-visible, [tabindex]:focus-visible
  { outline: 2px solid var(--lime-deep); outline-offset: 2px; }
```

`404.html` defined no `:focus-visible` rule at all. Its sixteen links fell back
to the user agent's own ring, measured at `1px auto rgb(16, 16, 16)` against the
site's `2px solid rgb(79, 112, 0)` with a 2px offset, so a keyboard user met a
different and unbranded focus indicator on exactly one page of the site.

It is not a contrast failure, which is why no earlier pass caught it: Chrome's
default ring is perfectly visible on `#f6f6f1`. It is a consistency failure, and
the same shape as the `.short-answer` defect of 10-31 and the `.article-dek`
defect of 10-21, markup shipped ahead of the stylesheet. The house rule was
added to 404.html's stylesheet, scoped to the elements that page actually has,
and verified by tabbing the page: all three focusable elements now resolve to
`2px solid rgb(79, 112, 0)` at a 2px offset.

### 3. The one real essay had no card on the homepage

The homepage article grid carries one `a.card` per dispatch. 91 of the 92
dispatches had one. `the-death-of-the-drag-and-drop-builder.html`, the lead
essay, had none.

It was not orphaned, which is why the orphan and inbound-link checks stayed
quiet: it is linked from the featured card and twice from the `#tags` cloud. The
cost is elsewhere. The grid's type filter reads the cards, so the pill set
`all / research / essays / post-mortems / tactics / interviews` resolves against
them, and the `essays` pill surfaced three cards and not this one. A reader
filtering the homepage for essays was shown three cards for essays that were
never published and not the single real essay the site has.

A card was added, tagged `essay`, carrying the essay's own figures: 4 min from
its `timeRequired` of `PT4M`, MAY 28 from its `datePublished` of 2026-05-28,
A. Rao from its author node, and its own dek. It was placed with the other
essay-tagged cards inside the *more dispatches* disclosure, and the
disclosure's own count was advanced from 69 to 70 to match what it now holds.
The `essays` filter now returns four cards, one of which opens.

### 4. The invariant table in `_tools/README.md` had two rows on one line

The run that fired during this pass appended its new section-heading `id` row to
the invariant table without a preceding newline, so that row and the
hero-masthead row above it became a single 1,785-character line carrying six
pipes. Every other row in the table sits on its own line with three or four. In
a two-column Markdown table the extra cells are dropped, so the invariant the
run had just added was documented nowhere a reader would see it. The line was
split at the row boundary; the table is 47 rows and every one is well formed.

## Four invariants added

All four defects above are invisible to a checker that reads fields, which is
why none of the forty-odd invariants already in `seo-check.py` caught them.
All four are now checked. Each was confirmed to fire on its defect and to pass
once the defect is fixed, by reintroducing each defect in turn and running the
checker against it.

**`404.html`'s *Latest dispatches* list holds exactly the ten newest
dispatches.** The check reads every dispatch's `article:published_time` and
issue number, sorts, and requires the list and the true top ten to be the same
set. It reports a dispatch that is missing and a dispatch that is stale
separately, because the prepend-and-drop mechanism produces them together.

**Every rendered page carries a `:focus-visible` rule that sets an outline.**
The generalisable form of defect 2, and of the `.short-answer` and
`.article-dek` defects before it.

**Every dispatch has an `a.card` in the homepage article grid.** The
generalisable form of defect 3. The distinction the check draws is between an
`a.card`, which opens, and an `article.card`, which does not.

**The homepage ticker's `N NEW DISPATCH(ES) THIS MONTH` equals the number of
dispatches published in the current calendar month.** The ticker read
`15 NEW DISPATCHES THIS MONTH` when this pass began. October had held 17, not
15, so the claim had been wrong inside October as well; and by 2026-11-02
*this month* had become November, which held none. The day's run recomputed it
to `1 NEW DISPATCH THIS MONTH`, which is true, so this one was corrected by the
run rather than by this pass and only the check is new.

The check anchors on today rather than on the newest dispatch's month, because
*this month* is the reader's month and not the archive's. One consequence is
deliberate and should be understood before anyone softens it: the check will
fire on the first of a month and stay red until that month's first dispatch
ships. That is correct. On those days the claim really is false. A run that
finds it red and is not itself publishing should rewrite the span to the true
count rather than widen the check.

All four are documented in `_tools/README.md` alongside the rest.

## Still open, not fixed here

**Seven cards on the homepage describe dispatches that do not exist.** Inside
the *more dispatches* disclosure sit seven `article.card` blocks carrying
titles, tags, read times, deks, dates and a byline for *M. Chen*, who has no
author page, for articles that were never published: *Triggers are the new
APIs*, *The night our retry logic ate itself*, *Seven prompts that compile
cleaner playbooks*, *Branching is a writing problem*, *We deleted 400 Zapier
zaps in a weekend*, *A taxonomy of "the workflow broke"* and *On naming things
the AI will read later*. An eighth `article.card` is a pull quote and is
decorative by design.

They are not broken links, and that was checked rather than assumed: they are
`<article>` rather than `<a>`, the stylesheet scopes `cursor: pointer` and the
hover lift to `a.card` and `.card[data-nav]`, and the phantoms compute to
`cursor: auto` with no lift and no lime title. Nothing invites a click. They
read as register entries, the same way the six-row back-catalogue list of
issues Nº 036 to Nº 041 does.

What they do affect is the filter. After fix 3 the pills resolve to 91
clickable research cards, 1 clickable essay, and then 3 essays, 1 post-mortem,
2 tactics and 1 interview that cannot be opened. Three of the six pills still
lead to nothing a reader can read.

This was left alone on the 10-31 precedent. That pass found the first
`.stat-num` claiming 137 dispatches against 91 pages, read it against its own
history, concluded it tracks the issue series rather than the page count, and
advanced it with the series rather than rewriting it, on the ground that
changing it "would have been an editorial decision about what the blog claims,
not a QA fix". The same reasoning holds here, and more strongly: removing seven
cards and three filter pills, or writing the seven articles, are both decisions
about what the blog is, and neither belongs to an unattended pass. The resolution
needs an editorial call, and the options are to publish the pieces, to retire
the cards and the pills that depend on them, or to mark the back catalogue as
what the archive list already is. Flagged, measured, and left for a human.
