**Dispatch Nº 89 - "The customer you can't ban"**
`how-to-block-a-customer-on-shopify.html` · by Priya Singh · 2026-07-23 · 2,290 words

Topic: blocking a specific customer from placing orders (serial returners, item-not-received
chargeback filers, abusive customers, competitors placing fake orders). Chosen over the
carry-forward candidates (cross-product BOGO, loyalty points, multi-store sync) because it fits
the brief best: forum demand is old, steady, and verbatim ("How can I block or blacklist certain
customers from placing orders?", "How can I block or blacklist a problematic customer in
Shopify?", "IP ban customer from buying from or even seeing my store?", "Block Customer,"
"blocking customers," and a 2024/2025-era "How to block customer account"), while third-party
coverage is thin: one aggregator listicle plus app listings, far less than multi-store sync,
which has substantial vendor content (Syncio, Webgility, EGNITION and others). No existing
dispatch covers it: the closest posts sit adjacent (high-risk orders judges the payment, card
testing handles bot floods, limit-quantity handles resellers, duplicate customers does identity
in the opposite direction), and none addresses refusing a known-bad person.

The native gap is real and specific: (1) no block or ban feature anywhere in the admin;
(2) deleting or disabling a customer account only affects the login, guest checkout stays open
to anyone with an email address; (3) fraud analysis targets stolen payment methods, so a serial
returner paying with their own valid card reads as low risk; (4) the standard Flow workaround
(tag customer blocked, Order created trigger, cancel + restock) fires only after the order
exists and the payment is captured, processing fees are generally not returned on refunds, the
condition is a literal string match that plus-addressed aliases and fresh emails walk past, and
nothing stops the next checkout; (5) checkout validation functions can genuinely decline a
checkout but require a custom app and a developer; (6) IP blocking apps fail against VPNs and
overblock shared addresses. The judgement framing: a block is about a person, not a string;
enforcement has to be identity-based; and false positives (canceling a good customer as a
suspect) cost more than misses, which is why borderline matches go to a human.

The pain-point stub heading screams the use case ("the customer you can't ban"); the H1 carries
the high-intent search phrasing ("How to block a customer on Shopify from placing orders").
Target phrases: "Shopify block customer," "ban customer Shopify," "Shopify blacklist customer,"
"block customer from ordering Shopify," "Shopify serial returner," "Shopify block email address
checkout," "Shopify IP block customer," "stop customer from buying Shopify," "Shopify Flow
cancel order blocked customer," "refund abuse Shopify."
