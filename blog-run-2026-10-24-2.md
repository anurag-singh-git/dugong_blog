## New post

**Nº 135 - "How to catch Shopify inventory transfers that never get received"**
`how-to-catch-shopify-inventory-transfers-that-never-get-received.html` - Priya Singh -
2026-10-24 - 3,560 words - 10 min read

Pain point: a pallet lands, somebody stacks the boxes, and nobody clicks Receive. From that moment
the store is holding stock it cannot sell, and nothing in Shopify is wrong. The transfer sits at
In progress, which is a healthy status, and no part of the admin counts the days. Three weeks later
a best seller turns out to have been sold out on the website since the day its stock arrived.

Why this topic: high demand, almost no content, and a mechanism nobody has written down. The open
web has app marketing and two help pages that each answer half the question. Nobody has put
together that Shopify's own inventory-states definitions place an unreceived transfer outside
Available and outside On hand simultaneously, that IN_PROGRESS is defined to cover both a shipment
underway and one already received so the status cannot separate "left this morning" from "landed
three weeks ago", that Flow's only two transfer triggers both fire on the happy path, that no
webhook topic can fire on a transfer that stops changing status, or that totalQuantity minus
receivedQuantity is the one number that answers the question and appears nowhere in the admin.

Facts used, all checked live this run:
- Help Center, Inventory states: incoming inventory is "on its way to your location from inventory
  transfers or apps" and "isn't available to sell until it has been received and its state has been
  changed to Available"; available inventory is "inventory that you can sell" and "doesn't include
  inventory that's considered Incoming"; on hand is "all inventory units that you have at a
  location" and "is made up of the total of your Committed, Unavailable, and Available inventory".
  Those three sentences read together are the article's central fact: an unreceived transfer is
  outside every number the storefront sells against.
- Help Center, Receive inventory: "After all items are received, the transfer status changes to
  Transferred"; "If you only receive some items, then the transfer remains In progress until you
  receive the remaining items"; a rejected line "is recorded on the transfer, but doesn't change
  inventory quantities at any location". The last of those means a transfer can reach Transferred
  with nothing added to a shelf.
- Help Center, Transfers: transfers "move and track inventory between your store locations", offer
  "Receive full or partial inventory at the destination location", and the page says nothing about
  an expected or estimated arrival date anywhere.
- Shopify.dev, InventoryTransferStatus: six values. DRAFT "created but not yet finalized",
  READY_TO_SHIP "created, but not yet shipped", IN_PROGRESS "in progress, with a shipment currently
  underway or received", TRANSFERRED "completely received at the destination", CANCELED, and OTHER
  "Status not included in the current enumeration set".
- Shopify.dev, InventoryTransfer object: "Tracks the movement of InventoryItem objects between
  Location objects"; `totalQuantity` is `Int!`, "The total quantity of items being transferred";
  `receivedQuantity` is `Int!`, "The total quantity of items accepted, rejected, or marked as
  canceled in the transfer"; `dateCreated` is the only clock on the object; `origin` and
  `destination` are nullable `LocationSnapshot`, kept so historical data survives a location being
  renamed or deleted, each with an optional link to the live Location that is nil when that
  location is gone.
- Help Center, Shopify Flow triggers: 83 triggers in one section. Exactly two concern transfers,
  Inventory transfer ready to ship and Inventory transfer completed. Nothing for purchase orders,
  receiving or incoming inventory.
- Shopify.dev, inventory transfer event topics: field-shaped. Parent `inventoryTransfer.*` plus
  `destination`, `lineItems.*`, `lineItems.totalQuantity`, the metafield variants, `status` and
  `tags`. `inventoryTransfer.status` "Fires when the status field on the InventoryTransfer object
  changes", and field-level topics support the update action only.
- Shopify Community, 15 May 2025 (Ankit6), "Webhooks Not Triggering on Inventory Transfers":
  inventory levels moved in the admin while `inventory_levels/update` and `inventory_items/update`
  produced nothing at his endpoint. A 21 May reply said the new Transfers "does send the webhooks
  as expected". On 13 June he reported the test still did not fire and that Shopify Support had
  confirmed transfers do not trigger inventory webhooks and had logged a feature request. No
  accepted solution.
- Shopify Developer Community, 9 December 2025 (sshaw), "Inventory_transfers Webhook Behavior,
  Payloads, and Bugs?": subscribed to `inventory_transfers/add_items`, `/cancel` and `/complete`,
  received only `/cancel`, and it fired when the transfer was marked as received; also asked where
  the payload documentation for these topics lives, since they appear in the Admin API docs and
  their payloads do not appear on the webhooks page. He closed it himself on 27 July 2026 as a
  misconfiguration at his end. The documentation question was never answered.
- Shopify Developer Community, 8 May 2026 (sshaw), "Inventory Transfer Complete Webhook: Transfer
  Has No Destination": `destination` nil after an `inventory_transfers/complete` webhook, and later
  `origin` nil on transfers with status TRANSFERRED, often enough that deleted locations seemed an
  unlikely explanation. KyleG-Shopify, 25 May: expected, because per the 2026-01 API change
  transfers can be created without an origin or destination, so a nil `destination` is valid while
  a nil `destination.location` means the live Location is deleted or inaccessible to the app. The
  nil origins on completed transfers were not explained and the thread is still open.
- Shopify Community, 24 July 2026 (HectorGomez), "Purchase Order - Incoming Inventory not working":
  reports built on incoming quantities recommending stock already on order, "my reports keep telling
  me to procure what is already in process". lumine the same day: "incoming isn't derived from the
  PO at all" and "there is literally nothing for Shopify to count", plus no PurchaseOrder object in
  the Admin GraphQL API as of 2026-07. The thread's workaround is to create the linked transfer at
  confirmation and mark it in transit before anything ships. On 27 July HectorGomez found that
  transfers do not update when the purchase order changes. No staff reply, no accepted answer.
- Shopify Community, 16 September 2026 (joeria), "Purchase Orders and Transfers": asks for linked
  transfers to update automatically when products are added to a purchase order, since the current
  flow "requires duplicate manual edits", and notes "there is no way back once it's marked as in
  transit". Kalvin_Mizzi's line in that thread, "one record per arrival", is quoted in the article
  as the rule the documentation does not state. No staff reply.

Playbook shipped: write down a lead time for every route; count unreceived units, not open
transfers; separate a transfer in transit from one that landed; chase the person at the destination,
not the buyer; group by the snapshot and keep a null bucket; close the partials, because the status
will not; send one list every morning, oldest money first. Honest limits stated in the post: no
automation can walk to the stockroom and count what is in the boxes; auto-receiving on a timer
writes unverified stock into the number the storefront sells against and is worse than the original
problem; a single-location store with no transfers needs none of this; and if receiving is nobody's
job in particular, the alert lands in an empty inbox.

Overlap handled deliberately: Nº 131 (2026-10-15) covers the purchase-order side of incoming and
cites the same 24 July thread. Nº 135 links to it in prose at the head of its own purchase-order
section rather than re-arguing it, and keeps its own weight on receiving, status semantics, the
webhook gap and the totalQuantity/receivedQuantity measurement.
