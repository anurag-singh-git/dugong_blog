# QA report, 2026-10-25

Scope: every rendered page on blog.dugong.live, checked for formatting, design,
mobile friendliness, readability and content. 103 rendered pages, 82 redirect
stubs, 90 dispatches, numbered 47 to 136. Pages were rendered in headless
Chromium at 320, 360, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS pixels.

Note on timing: the tree was quiet for 24 hours when this pass began at 10:46
UTC, and the day's publishing run fired at 10:59 while it was in progress,
shipping dispatch Nº 136 (`how-to-chase-overdue-shopify-b2b-invoices.html`) and
touching about 80 files. No edit of this pass was lost to it: the backups were
taken after 10:59, every fix was applied on top of the published state, and the
404 list and tag cloud were both confirmed to still carry the new dispatch
afterwards. The repository was then polled until it had been quiet for five
minutes, and the whole verification below, including the render pass, was
re-run from scratch against that settled tree with Nº 136 included.

Note on fonts: the webfont hosts are not reachable from either shell, so Hanken
Grotesk, Inter and JetBrains Mono were installed from their npm packages and
served to the renderer in place of the Google Fonts request. The measured
glyphs are the real ones, which is what the overflow and small-text numbers
depend on. The single console error on every page is that blocked request and
is an artefact of this environment, not of the site.

`_tools/seo-check.py` passed before this pass and passes after it.

## What was wrong, and what was done

### 1. The homepage tag cloud used three labels twice, for different dispatches

The `#tags` cloud is the only topical index of the whole archive the homepage
offers. Three of its labels pointed at two dispatches each, so the reader saw
the same word twice in one cloud with no way to tell the two apart. The cloud
encodes weight as font size, which makes the duplication worse than cosmetic:
`dimensional weight` appeared at 1.4rem and at 2.3rem simultaneously, so the
size contradicted itself about which topic mattered more. Same link text
pointing at two destinations is also WCAG 2.4.4.

In each pair the canonical article kept the label and the other was relabelled,
with the replacement taken from the article's own wording:

| Label | Kept by | Relabelled on | Now reads | Support in that article |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| split shipments | half-the-order-never-ships | route-orders-to-the-right-warehouse | closest location | "closest location" appears 8 times |
| dimensional weight | shipping-rates-that-ignore-product-dimensions (2.3rem) | shipping-label-adjustments | billed weight | "billed weight" appears twice; it is the mechanism the piece describes |
| order routing | route-orders-to-the-right-warehouse (2.2rem) | automatically-tag-orders | tag normalization | the piece is about Shopify reading VIP, vip and Vip as three tags; "normali*" appears 11 times, "casing" 4 |

### 2. Two live dispatches had no tag in that cloud at all

`how-to-edit-a-shopify-order-after-its-been-placed.html` (Nº 53) and
`the-death-of-the-drag-and-drop-builder.html` were absent from the cloud
entirely, while the other 87 dispatches carried one to three tags each. Both
are in the feed and the sitemap and neither is noindexed, so this was not the
deliberate exclusion that applies to the two rejected draft pages. The cloud is
written at publication, one short run of tags per dispatch, and no later run
goes back over it, which is the same failure shape as the READ NEXT drift found
on 10-21.

Four tags were added, again from each article's own language:

* order editing (1.7) and shipping address changes (1.3)
* drag-and-drop builders (1.6) and natural-language automation (1.2)

The cloud now holds 219 tags, 219 distinct labels, and covers all 90 dispatches.

### 3. Half the 404 page's dispatch list carried the site name in its link text

`404.html` builds its *Latest dispatches* list from each page's `<title>`. A
title keeps the ` | Dugong` suffix only when the headline is short enough to
leave room for it under the 60-character cap, so copying titles verbatim
produced a list where five of the ten entries read "How to handle return to
sender orders on Shopify | Dugong" and the other five did not. The suffix
belongs to the browser tab, never to a headline rendered inside the page. All
five were stripped; the list is now consistent, and no link text anywhere else
on the site carries the suffix.

The list itself was checked separately and is current and correctly ordered: it
holds exactly the ten newest dispatches, Nº 136 included.

## Checks added to `_tools/seo-check.py`

All three findings are things a publishing run writes once and never revisits,
so all three are now invariants. Each was confirmed to fire against the
pre-fix tree and to pass against the fixed one:

* no label in the `#tags` cloud points at two different dispatches
* every dispatch has at least one tag in the `#tags` cloud
* no link text inside a rendered page ends in the ` | Dugong` suffix

Run against the state this pass started from they report exactly the ten
defects fixed above and nothing else. `_tools/README.md` carries the reasons.

## Open, needs your decision, not fixed

### The newsletter signup records nothing and tells the reader it worked

The form in `index.html` has no `action`, its input has no `name`, and its
`onsubmit` calls `preventDefault()`, rewrites the button to `subscribed ✓` and
clears the field. There is no `fetch`, no XHR and no provider endpoint anywhere
in the file or the site. A reader who types an email is told they subscribed;
the address goes nowhere. The same block claims `4,200 readers`, which nothing
on the site supports.

This is the most serious thing found. It is left alone deliberately: the fix is
either wiring the form to a real provider or changing what it promises, and
both are your calls, not a QA pass's. It has been this way since the first
commit and no previous report has raised it.

### "135 dispatches published" on the homepage, against 90 on the site

The hero stat tracks the issue number rather than the archive. Dispatches run
Nº 47 to Nº 136, which is 90 pages. If 46 earlier dispatches really were
published and retired the figure is true and should be left; if it is just
mirroring the issue number it has been drifting upward on its own since the
beginning. Not changed, because only you know which. Note that the stat still
reads 135 while the newest dispatch is now Nº 136, so whatever it is meant to
count, it is already a day behind. The two stats beside it, `3.2M runs
observed` and `94% first-compile accuracy`, are product metrics this pass has
no way to verify.

### No page carries a print stylesheet

None of the 103 rendered pages define `@media print`. Printing a playbook, or
saving one as PDF, carries the nav, the scrolling ticker, the CTA band, the
signup form and the READ NEXT grid of eighty-odd cards along with the article.
For a site whose dispatches are meant to be followed at a desk this is worth
having, but adding it is a design change across every page rather than a defect
fix, so it is left as a recommendation.

## What was checked and found clean

* No horizontal scroll and no element past the viewport on any of 103 pages at
  320, 360, 390, 768 or 1280px. The only elements that extend past it are the
  off-screen skip link at -9999px and the ticker marquee, both by design.
* No clipped text anywhere. The card deks that look truncated are
  `-webkit-line-clamp` doing it deliberately, with an ellipsis. The two
  containers reporting clipped content, `.cta-band-inner` and `.signup-inner`,
  are clipping their own decorative `::before` glow, which is positioned at
  `right: -90px` and `right: -70px` on purpose.
* Contrast: zero failures against WCAG 1.4.3 on any non-decorative text at any
  of the five widths, compositing translucent backgrounds over their parents.
  The only text below 4.5:1 is the `·` byline divider at 3.03:1, which carries
  no information.
* Tap targets: zero under 24px, applying the WCAG 2.5.8 inline-link exemption.
* No text below 12px at any width up to and including 1024px.
* No page error, no load failure, no broken image, no image without `alt`.
* One `h1` per page, no heading-level skips, no duplicate element ids, `lang`
  set on every page, every `<title>` within 60 characters.
* Reduced motion: 102 of 103 pages carry a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`
  block that zeroes every animation and transition, which covers the infinite
  ticker marquee. 404.html has no such block and needs none; its only motion is
  a 0.2s hover colour transition.
* Focus: `:focus-visible` and a visible outline on 102 of 103 pages, and a skip
  link on every one.
* The signup form's input carries a real `<label for>`. No empty link, no
  generic "click here" or "read more" link text, and after the cloud fix no
  link text on any page that resolves to two different URLs.
* feed.xml and sitemap.xml both parse; every channel and item carries the
  required elements, guids are unique, every sitemap URL carries a well-formed
  `lastmod`, and no feed date is in the future.
* Markdown renditions: all 90 carry an h1, none leaks HTML, an HTML entity or
  an empty link.
* No `target="_blank"` anywhere, so no missing `rel="noopener"` is exploitable.
* House style: the only mixed spellings on the site resolve as correct.
  `canceled` appears 10 times and every one is inside a quoted Shopify UI or
  trigger name, where the single-l form is what Shopify writes. The two
  `Personalisation` are inside the quoted title of a Shopify community thread.
  `analyses` is the ordinary plural of analysis. `cancelled` stands at 258 for
  the reasons the 10-24 pass recorded.
* Nº 136, which shipped mid-pass, was checked against every finding class
  above: 51-character title, 2,967 words at 11 min (270 wpm, in band), present
  in md/, llms.txt and the feed, tagged in the cloud, and carrying no site-name
  suffix in any link text.
* Readability: 170 sentences across 13 dispatches run past 60 words. Every one
  checked is the house colon-then-parallel-list construction, which reads
  cleanly; left alone as voice, not error.

## Verification method note

The render pass ran twice: once against the tree as found, and again in full
against the settled tree after Nº 136 landed. Every edit was applied with a
strict exactly-one-occurrence assertion per replacement, and the first attempt
aborted without writing when one assertion failed, which is why `index.html`
was left untouched until the anchor was corrected. Backups of both edited files
as they stood before the fixes are in `_qa_tmp/run-10-25/`.
