## New post

**Nº 144 - "How to catch the Shopify products in the wrong shipping profile"**
`how-to-catch-shopify-products-in-the-wrong-shipping-profile.html` - Priya Singh - 2026-11-19 -
2,855 words - 10 min read

Pain point: you build one custom shipping profile, and from that day on every product you add
goes into the general profile instead and nothing moves it. The custom profile stops being
complete the day after you build it. The defect surfaces at checkout, either as no rate at all
for a destination you ship to weekly, or as the cheap general rate on an item that should have
carried freight. Neither direction raises anything in the admin.

Why this topic: high demand, five years of unresolved public complaining, and almost nothing
written about it as a catalog hygiene problem. What exists is Shopify's own shipping profiles
page, which is accurate and reads as reassurance, plus a long tail of agency posts about "no
shipping rates available at checkout" that treat it as a rate configuration error. Nobody has
written up the membership mechanism, which is the actual cause, or the fact that the migration
to shipping options by market moves the gap into a collection rather than closing it.

Facts used, all checked live this run:
- Shopify Help Center, Setting up shipping profiles: all existing and new products are added to
  the general profile; up to 99 custom profiles; a product or variant that is not in a custom
  profile uses the general shipping rates; the page's own example is fragile items; it notes
  profiles are moving to shipping options by market and that rates migrate automatically.
- Shopify Help Center, What is changing with shipping: "Shipping is moving from shipping profiles
  to Markets"; shipping zones and location groups are replaced; "Each rate uses product
  conditions to set which products it applies to"; product based profiles become unpublished
  collections prefixed `[Shipping]`, e.g. `[Shipping] Fragile`, assigned automatically to the
  right product conditions; rates sharing a shipping option name are consolidated; within one
  option "only the highest matching rate is charged, instead of the rates being added together";
  split shipping is activated for every upgraded store including those that had deactivated it;
  rollout is gradual with no dates, there is an opt out window, and once it closes you cannot
  switch back.
- Shopify Community, "Why are new products missing from my General Shipping Profile?" (July
  2022): several newly added products absent from the only profile on the store, requires
  shipping ticked on all of them, stocking location correct, imported through Printful. Replies
  asked for screenshots and raised active versus draft. No resolution.
- Shopify Community, "No products under the general shipping profile" (September 2021): the one
  authoritative statement of the mechanism, from Shopify Social Care. Products not explicitly
  added to a custom profile default to General, "a product cannot be in two shipping profiles at
  the same time", so assigning a product to a custom profile removes it from General. The
  merchant was simultaneously reporting that English customers were told their location is not
  available for shipping. Nobody joined the two halves. Closed unresolved.
- Shopify Community, "Shipping Problems for a New Product" (June to July 2025): first self
  stocked product, new profile with discounted USPS and DHL zones, "shipping not available to
  this address" on every test, other countries' postal codes no help. Merchant: "I dont really
  know what to do at this point." The one diagnostic datum in the thread: "Flat rate tested and
  it worked, so it must be a problem with calculated rates." A later reply suggests removing the
  product from General and adding named carrier services. No confirmation it worked.
- Shopify Community, "Newly added shipping rates not showing at checkout" (July 2022): an Irish
  store adds flat UK and Northern Ireland rates, every British customer blocked with a
  non-delivery message, two weeks of screenshots, diagnosis "the products were not added, or the
  conditions were mismatched", recommendation to collapse back to one general profile holding all
  products. No confirmed fix.
- Shopify Community, "Adding products or variants to a shipping profile with api" (October 2025):
  `productUpdate` with `deliveryProfileId` returns "InputObject 'ProductInput' doesn't accept
  argument 'deliveryProfileId'"; the working answer is `deliveryProfileUpdate` with
  `variantsToAssociate`, confirmed by the original poster. Association is per variant.
- Shopify Dev, DeliveryProfile object and deliveryProfiles query, GraphQL Admin API:
  `profileItems`, `productVariantsCount`, `activeMethodDefinitionsCount`,
  `locationsWithoutRatesCount`, `zoneCountryCount`, `unassignedLocations`, and `merchantOwnedOnly`
  on the query. So the API can answer the membership and coverage question the admin cannot.

Playbook shipped: pull every merchant owned profile with its variants, location groups and zones
daily; flag any product in general that resembles the contents of a custom profile on type,
vendor, weight, tags and title pattern; walk each profile for countries with no active rate and
locations with no rates at all; decide by sibling agreement rather than by rule, and escalate
disagreement as one line with evidence; assign per variant because the API is per variant, and
always send removals to a person because that is the direction that breaks a working checkout;
quote the failing destination from the real stock location after assigning, because a rate in a
profile and a rate at checkout are not the same claim; and report the gap the day it opens, since
the same gap found in March is a quarter of mispriced freight and a quarter of silent abandons.

Overlap handled deliberately: Nº 118 (dimensional weight) owns the rate that is correctly
configured and wrong for the parcel, Nº 119 (state restrictions) owns Shopify's unit of shipping
not matching the merchant's, and the address validation dispatch owns the destination rather than
the product. This post keeps its weight on membership, which none of them touch, and on the fact that
the native remedy offered in public is to stop having more than one profile.
