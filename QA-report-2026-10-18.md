# QA report, 2026-10-18

Scope: every rendered page on blog.dugong.live (97 pages after the morning
publishing run, 85 dispatches), checked for formatting, design, mobile
friendliness, readability and content accuracy. Pages were rendered in
headless Chromium at 320, 360, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS pixels with the real
Hanken Grotesk / Inter / JetBrains Mono webfonts loaded, not fallbacks.

`_tools/seo-check.py` passed before this run and passes after it.

## What was wrong, and what was done

### 1. The header nav disappeared on every phone and tablet

`@media (max-width: 820px) { .nav-links a:not(.nav-cta) { display: none; } }`
hid Latest, Topics and Archive below 820px and put nothing in their place: no
menu button, no disclosure, nothing. Every reader on a phone or a tablet in
portrait had the brand mark and the CTA and no way into the site. There is no
JavaScript menu anywhere in the repository, so this was not a broken toggle,
it was a nav that had simply been switched off at small widths.

Fixed with CSS only. Below 820px the header wraps: brand and CTA on the first
row, the three links on a second row at 13.5px, tightened to 13px below 380px.
No JavaScript added, no markup added, nothing to go wrong with scripts off.

### 2. Every tap target in the header and footer was under 24px

The 2026-10-10 pass added `padding` to `.nav-links a` and `.foot-cols a`, but
put it inside `@media (max-width: 560px)`. WCAG 2.5.8 is not viewport
conditional, so from 561px up the links stayed at 23.2px (header) and 23.5px
(footer) on every page that carries the shared chrome.

`min-height: 24px` now applies at every width.

### 3. The marker highlight sat under the words instead of through them

`.article-title em` and `.hero-title em` drew the lime band from 62% to 96% of
the inline box. Hanken Grotesk puts the baseline at 76.9% of that box
(measured, ascent 1.00em, descent 0.30em), so the band ran from mid-glyph to
a fifth of an em below the baseline: it read as a thick underline sliding off
the bottom of the text and clipped the descenders of g, y and p. Visible on
every dispatch title on the site and on the homepage hero.

Band moved to 57%-81%, which runs through the lower half of the letters and
stops just under the baseline. The homepage hero also still used the old
absolutely-positioned `::after` bar, which sat lower still; it now uses the
same gradient treatment as the dispatch titles.

### 4. READ NEXT had grown to 79 cards

The related-posts grid at the foot of each dispatch is appended to, never
trimmed, so its size tracks the age of the post: the newest dispatches carry
3 cards and the oldest carry 79. On a 390px phone that block measured 28,000px
tall, 56-60% of the entire page, sitting between the end of the article and
the footer.

The first 6 cards stay in the grid. The rest move into a `<details>`
disclosure labelled "N more dispatches in the archive". Every link is still in
the HTML and still crawlable; nothing was deleted. Worst-case page height on a
phone fell from 49,809px to about 25,000px, and the median page from 35,095px
to 21,632px.

One real bug came with it: cards inside a closed `<details>` never intersect
the viewport, so the IntersectionObserver that clears `.reveal` never fires
and they would have opened at `opacity: 0`. `.related-more .reveal` now
opts out of the reveal animation.

### 5. Homepage card summaries were whole article openings

75 of the 90 `card-dek` paragraphs on index.html held 170 to 515 words, the
entire opening of the dispatch rather than a summary. `-webkit-line-clamp: 5`
hid the overflow, so the visible result was five lines cut off mid-sentence,
and the other 485 words shipped to every visitor anyway. The site's own
convention, which every hub page follows, is that the card dek is the target
page's meta description.

Every dek over 45 words (75 on the homepage, about 220 more in the related
grids on 69 dispatch pages) now carries the target page's meta description.
Deks under 45 words were left alone: those are deliberate editorial variants,
not defects. index.html dropped from 342KB to 257KB.

### 6. Five hub and author pages were printing stale numbers

The daily publishing run updates card lists and most counts, but has now
repeatedly left the "FIRST -> LAST" date line behind. As found today:

| Page | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| shopify-automation-questions.html | 82 dispatches | 84 |
| shopify-inventory-automation.html | 16 dispatches, JUN 05 -> OCT 12 | 17, JUN 05 -> OCT 15 |
| shopify-shipping-and-fulfillment-automation.html | JUN 04 -> SEP 19 | JUN 04 -> OCT 18 |
| shopify-customer-and-wholesale-automation.html | JUN 14 -> OCT 10 | JUN 14 -> OCT 17 |
| priya-singh.html | MAY 28 -> OCT 14 | MAY 28 -> OCT 18 |

`_qa_tmp/run-10-18/fix_stale_meta.py` recomputes all of these from the
dispatches each page actually links, rather than hard-coding them, so it can
be re-run after any publishing run.

### 7. Three footer links all went to the same unfiltered page

On all 94 non-homepage pages the footer's read column linked Latest, Essays,
Post-mortems and Interviews to a bare `index.html`. The homepage's own footer
already pre-selects the matching filter through `data-filter-link`, so the
behaviour existed and had never been extended to the dispatch pages.

The three filter pills on index.html gained ids (`#essays`, `#post-mortems`,
`#interviews`), the homepage applies the matching filter from the hash on load
and on `hashchange`, and the footers now link to those anchors. The anchors
resolve, so the cross-file anchor check still passes.

### 8. Repairs to today's publishing run

The 2026-10-18 dispatch shipped a 198-character meta description against a
70-165 house limit (shortened to 152, with og: and twitter: kept in step), and
four pages that gained the new dispatch had their sitemap `lastmod` moved to
2026-10-18 while their `dateModified` stayed on the 16th or 17th. Both are
exactly the failure modes `_tools/seo-check.py` exists to catch; the checker
caught them.

### 9. Smaller things

* `.faq-a, .article-dek, .card-dek { overflow-wrap: anywhere; }` was added on
  2026-10-13 to 5 pages. It is now on all 97.
* "refills to its pre sale count" -> "pre-sale".

## What was checked and found clean

* No horizontal scroll, clipped content or unscrollable wide block on any of
  97 pages at 320, 360, 390 or 768px.
* No contrast failure against WCAG AA anywhere, text or large text.
* No broken internal link, in-page anchor or cross-file anchor.
* All 97 pages parse without a single HTML5 error.
* One `h1` per page, no heading-level skips.
* No console errors, no page errors, no broken images (the site uses no
  `<img>` at all).
* No em or en dashes, no mojibake, no placeholder text, no doubled words, no
  unbalanced quotes or parentheses in any visible copy.
* Spelling: 7,754 distinct words checked; every unknown token is domain
  vocabulary (WISMO, metafield, chargeback, ShopifyQL) or a deliberate
  in-quote misspelling ("gmial.com" in the confirmation-email dispatch).
* Mean sentence length 19.0 words across the site. 77 sentences run past 75
  words, all of them deliberate list-form sentences in the "the compiler fills
  in the rest" passages.
* `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured on all 96 animated pages.
* Both local font stacks, the ticker marquee and the `<pre id="playbook">`
  blocks behave correctly down to 320px.

## Known, deliberate, left alone

* The four inline links in the 404 page's closing sentence measure 17px tall.
  WCAG 2.5.8 exempts targets whose size is constrained by the line-height of
  the sentence they sit in.
* The footer's product and company columns point seven differently-labelled
  links at `https://dugong.live`. Fixing that needs the real destination URLs
  on the marketing site, which this pass cannot verify. Worth a look.
* `131 dispatches published` on the homepage against 85 dispatch pages. This
  tracks the issue number (ISSUE Nº 131), not the page count, and is
  consistent with the masthead, so it was left as written.
