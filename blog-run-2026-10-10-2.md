**Nº 123 - "The sale that starts the day after they buy"**
`how-to-handle-price-adjustment-requests-on-shopify.html` · Priya Singh · 2026-10-10 · ~1,820 words incl. FAQ · 7 min

Pain point: a sale goes live and customers who bought days earlier email asking for the difference back. Support does the arithmetic and the partial refund by hand, every promo, with no policy written down.

Facts used, all checked live this run:
- Shopify Community, April 2025, "Looking for a smoother way to handle price difference refunds after a promotion": OP calculates the difference after each promo and issues partial refunds by hand, asks whether anyone has found a more efficient or automated way. One reply, two clarifying questions, thread still open.
- Flow triggers reference: no price trigger anywhere in the product group. Community thread (Sept 2022 onward) "There is no Shopify event for 'price updated'", with a Shopify staff reply on 6 Oct 2026 saying a product updated trigger covering selected fields including price is in limited testing, no GA announced.
- Flow actions reference: no refund action, no create gift card action, no store credit action. Refund created exists only as a trigger. Writes go through Send Admin API request.
- Refunding orders help page: editable Refund amount field in the Summary, deselectable Restock items, further partial refunds up to the total available, destination can be original payment method, store credit, or both.
- Shopify Payments refunds page: credit card fees are not returned on a refund; third-party gateway in a Shopify Payments country also gets nothing back; fees are refunded proportionally only on a third-party gateway where Shopify Payments is unavailable, and only for admin refunds. Refunds generally possible up to 120 days, subject to the customer's bank.

Why it won: demand is seasonal and large (every sale, every BFCM) and page one is Amazon, Costco and Staples price-adjustment policies plus one independent consultant post. No editorial piece explains the Shopify-specific mechanics, the discount-allocation arithmetic, or why a price-drop rule without classification is dangerous.
Considered and dropped: serial returner / return abuse detection (vendor editorial already thick: Signifyd, Loop, Riskified, Cahoot), subscription dunning and failed payment recovery (well covered by subscription app vendors), partial fulfilment on a single out-of-stock line (carried forward).
